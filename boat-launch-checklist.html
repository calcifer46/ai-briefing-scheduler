🏠 Phase 1 — At Home / Before You Leave
  • Check weather & wind forecast
    Aim for winds under 15 mph for a small aluminum jon boat. Check lightning risk.
  • Tell someone your float plan
    Which lake, which ramp, expected return time, who to call if you're overdue.
  • Count & inspect life jackets (PFDs)
    One properly-sized, Coast Guard-approved PFD per person on board. Wearable throwable for boats >16 ft.
  • Check fire extinguisher
    Required on any motorized boat. Gauge should be in the green zone.
  • Check fuel level & bring extra
    Rule of thirds: 1/3 out, 1/3 back, 1/3 reserve. A portable 6-gal tank is handy backup.
  • Check 2-stroke oil level / mix ratio
    Mercury 25hp (2-stroke): verify premix ratio per your manual (typically 50:1 at normal use). Low oil = engine death.
  • Inspect propeller
    Look for nicks, cracks, bent blades, and fishing line wrapped around the shaft.
  • Charge / test battery (if applicable)
    Even if you use a pull-start, check any trolling-motor or fish-finder batteries.
  • Pack safety kit: first aid, whistle, flashlight, anchor & line
    Also bring a bailing bucket or manual bilge pump — aluminum boats can take on spray.
  • Check & secure trailer hitch & coupler
    Coupler locked, safety chains crossed under the tongue, breakaway cable (if equipped) attached.
  • Test trailer lights (brake, turn, running)
    Have someone stand behind while you operate the vehicle controls, or use a 4-pin tester.
  • Check trailer tire pressure & condition
    Check both tires plus the spare. Don't forget — trailer tires lose pressure sitting.
  • Verify all tie-down straps are secure
    Bow strap to winch, stern transom straps. Bow safety chain if present.
  • Motor tilted up & transom saver in place
    Prevents road vibration from damaging the tilt mechanism while towing.
  • Locate the drain plug Critical
    Know where it is BEFORE you leave. You'll need it at the ramp. Keep a spare in the truck glovebox.
🚗 Phase 2 — At the Ramp (Before Backing In)
  • Pull into the staging area — not the ramp lane
    Do all prep here so you're not blocking others while working on the boat.
  • Install the drain plug Critical
    This is the #1 beginner mistake. Reach under the transom and thread it in firmly. Check it twice.
  • Remove motor cover / engine cover (if any)
    Remove any canvas or tarp covering the motor before launch.
  • Remove transom saver & tilt motor down
    Motor should be trimmed down to near-vertical for launch depth on ramp.
  • Disconnect trailer lights plug
    Dunking hot trailer lights causes bulb failure and corrosion. Unplug before the wheels enter the water.
  • Remove stern tie-down straps
    Remove both rear straps now. Leave the bow winch strap on until the boat is floating.
  • Attach dock lines / bow rope
    Have a long rope ready on the bow cleat so you can control the boat after it floats off.
  • Load all gear into the boat
    Load tackle, rods, cooler, etc. in the staging area — easier than dockside.
🚤 Phase 3 — Launching
  • Back trailer slowly down the ramp
    Go slow. Stop before the tow vehicle's rear tires enter the water if possible.
  • Apply parking brake & chock wheels
    Engage park brake before getting out of the vehicle.
  • Release bow winch strap (hold the rope!)
    Keep control of the bow line so the boat doesn't drift away.
  • Float boat off trailer
    Gently push or let water float the boat free. Tie to dock cleat or have a partner hold the line.
  • Pull truck & empty trailer forward, park
    Clear the ramp for other boaters before doing anything else.
  • Start the engine at the dock
    Squeeze fuel bulb until firm, set choke if cold, shift to neutral, start. Never run a 2-stroke dry.
  • Verify tell-tale (water-pump stream) is flowing Critical
    A small stream of water should squirt out of the indicator hole on the lower unit within 30 sec of starting. No stream = shut down immediately — your impeller may be failing.
  • Check bilge / floor for water intrusion
    A little is normal; steady rising water means the drain plug is loose or missing.
  • Attach & don kill-switch lanyard
    Clip the engine kill switch lanyard to yourself any time you're underway. Tiller boats require this.
  • All passengers seated & PFDs on / accessible
    Verify everyone is seated before leaving the no-wake zone.
🌊 Phase 4 — On the Water (Periodic Checks)
  • Observe no-wake zone & speed limits
    Most ramp areas enforce idle speed within 100–200 ft of shore or marked buoys.
  • Re-check tell-tale stream after 5 minutes
    Confirm cooling water is still flowing once up on plane.
  • Scan bilge every 30–60 minutes
    Aluminum boats can flex; rivet leaks are possible. More water than normal = investigate.
  • Monitor fuel — watch the gauge or tank
    Rule of thirds. Start heading back when you've used ~1/3 of your fuel.
  • Watch weather — head in if storm threatens
    Lightning on open water is extremely dangerous. Give storms a wide berth — head to shore early.
🔄 Phase 5 — Loading / Retrieving at the Ramp
  • Idle into the dock / loading area slowly
    Come in at low speed, into the wind or current if possible.
  • Pull truck & trailer to staging area first
    Do NOT back down the ramp until you're ready to load — clear the ramp fast.
  • Back trailer into water — just deep enough to float boat on
    The bow roller should be at or just below water level.
  • Drive boat onto trailer, cut engine
    Approach straight and slow. Kill the engine just before the bow touches the winch post.
  • Attach bow winch strap & crank bow snug
    Hook winch strap to bow eye. Crank until bow is seated firmly on the bow roller.
  • Pull truck & trailer out of water
    Pull forward slowly and straight. Stop in the staging area — not on the ramp.
  • Remove drain plug to drain bilge water Critical
    Remove it now while water can drain freely. Storing with the plug in traps water — corrosion and added weight. Many states require the plug to be out before leaving the ramp (invasive species rules).
  • Tilt motor up & lock; re-install transom saver
    Protect the motor and tilt system during transport.
  • Attach all stern tie-down straps
    Secure at least two stern straps in addition to the bow winch strap.
  • Re-connect trailer lights plug
    Plug back in and verify lights before heading onto the road.
  • Do a walk-around before leaving the ramp
    Motor up, all straps on, hitch locked, chains crossed, safety cable attached.
✅ Phase 6 — Post-Trip / At Home
  • Flush motor with fresh water (if on saltwater or if recommended)
    Even on fresh water, a quick flush removes algae and debris. Use a flushing muff on the lower unit intakes; run for 5–10 min.
  • Rinse the boat hull & trailer
    Required in many states to prevent invasive species transport. Rinse all live wells too.
  • Spray motor with corrosion inhibitor (CorrosionX / Boeshield)
    A light spray on exposed metal extends engine life, especially the lower unit and linkages.
  • Tilt motor back down after flush & let water drain
    Tilting down drains any remaining water from the powerhead and cooling passages. Then tilt back up for storage.
  • Top off fuel or add fuel stabilizer if storing
    For storage >30 days: use stabilizer and run the engine to distribute it. Full tanks reduce condensation.
  • Rinse & dry life jackets; repack safety gear
    PFDs degrade if stored wet. Air-dry before stowing. Replenish any first-aid supplies used.
  • Store drain plug in a known location Important
    Put it in the same spot every time — glove box, cup holder, or a hook on the boat. A lost plug will ruin your day.
  • Note anything to fix / replace before next trip
    Write it down now while it's fresh — low oil, a loose fitting, a dying light bulb, etc.
